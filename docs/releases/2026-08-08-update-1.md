# CODE Portal, Update 1

The first big update since the portal launched. Events and points are the headline: how events are created, how attendance is taken, and how points are awarded have all been rebuilt around things officers can configure themselves, instead of settings that needed a developer.

---

## Events and the calendar

**Multi-day and all-day events.** An event can now run across several days and shows as a single bar spanning them on the calendar, rather than needing one event per day.

**Event types are yours to define.** Casual, official, birthday and any others are now managed in the admin console. Each carries its own label, colour and ordering, and you decide which roles are allowed to create which type. Types can be retired without breaking events that already use them.

**Colour-coded calendar.** Events take the colour of their type, so a month tells you what kind of month it is at a glance.

**Share links.** Every event has a short link you can post anywhere. Opening it shows the event without needing to sign in first. There is also a QR code for the link, and an "Add to Google Calendar" button plus an `.ics` download for everything else.

**Signup forms.** Events can collect answers when someone signs up, with per-event control over whether responses are visible to everyone or only to organisers.

**Informational events.** Mark an event as informational and it appears on the calendar with no signup and no check-in. Anything already collected is kept, not deleted.

**A faster calendar.** Switching between Calendar and List, or moving between months, now responds immediately instead of pausing on every click.

---

## Taking attendance

**A proper mobile scanner.** Full screen, with a torch toggle, camera flip, and a running count. Every scan gives a clear result: a green confirmation with the member's photo, an amber warning for someone already scanned, or a red rejection, each with its own sound and vibration.

**Undo.** Scanned the wrong person? Undo it on the spot. Their attendance and any points it awarded are both removed.

**A recent-scans list**, so you can check what has gone through without leaving the scanner.

**Grace periods.** Each event can set how late still counts as on time, and attendance is marked accordingly rather than by a single fixed rule.

**Check-in QR.** The check-in card can now switch between your member code and a QR of the event's share link, so an organiser can project the link or scan a member from the same place.

---

## Points

Points used to be a single number attached to an event. They are now a configurable system.

**Point types.** Define as many as you need, each with its own label and rules, and choose which ones count toward retention.

**Per-event awards.** An event can award several point types at once, each with its own value.

**Retired types are preserved.** Turning a point type off stops it being awarded from then on. It does not erase points already earned.

**A breakdown on your profile.** Instead of one total, you see where your points came from, split by type.

**Leaderboards by type**, so a ranking can reflect a specific kind of contribution rather than one combined score.

---

## Admin console

**Events and points split into six focused screens** instead of one long page.

**School years.** Terms are managed in the console, with start and end dates, retention thresholds, and a check that stops two terms overlapping.

**Roles and access, rebuilt.** One search box now covers everyone: it filters the admin list as you type, then looks through every other member, including those who are pending or inactive, and offers to add them directly. No second search to find.

**Grant a role before someone joins.** Give an invited person a role using their email, before they have ever signed in. The role applies automatically the first time they log in, so they arrive able to do their job rather than waiting on another admin.

**Member management.** Search across the whole roster, bulk invite by email, and audit entries now record who an action was performed *on*, not just who performed it.

---

## Link shortener

**Readable click charts.** Hover any point on the clicks-over-time chart to see the exact date and count, with a crosshair marking where you are. The chart is keyboard accessible too: focus it and use the arrow keys.

**Traffic breakdown.** Hovering a slice of the source or device chart highlights it along with its legend row and shows the count in the middle.

---

## Profile

**Edit your own details** — name, nickname, pronouns, batch and birthday — with a clear view and edit mode, and errors that actually tell you what went wrong.

**Birthday visibility is yours to choose.** Opt out and your birthday stops appearing on the shared calendar.

---

## Fixes worth calling out

- **Editing an all-day or multi-day event no longer turns it into a timed event.** Changing anything, even just the title, used to silently wipe the setting. There is now also a control to switch an existing event to or from all-day, which previously could only be set at creation.
- **Sign-in works for every approved domain**, not just the first one configured.
- **Scan records are written atomically**, so a scan can no longer half-succeed.
- **The scanner explains itself** when no school year is active, instead of failing without a reason.
- **Retention totals agree** between your profile and the leaderboard.
- **Long labels stay inside the screen on mobile** across the points and event screens.
- **Invited members keep their details** when they sign in for the first time.
- **The colour dot shows on multi-day event bars.**

## Under the hood

Nothing here changes what you see, but it is why the update is safe to ship.

- The database migration history was unified into a single sequence shared by every environment, replacing two parallel histories that had drifted apart. Every change is now rehearsed on a staging copy of production before production sees it.
- Automated checks confirm the database structure matches what the application expects, and that data survives a migration. Both run on every change.
- Unfinished sections are held behind switches that fail closed: if a setting is missing or malformed, the section stays hidden rather than appearing half-built.
- The test suite grew from 209 to 488 tests.
